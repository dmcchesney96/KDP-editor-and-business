# Slow With Jesus — Market & Launch Playbook

## Scope

Project-specific marketing guidance for the `Slow With Jesus` Gospel series.

This file does **not** replace the reusable KDP skills in this repository. It supplies the approved positioning and experiment rules that those skills should use when working on this series.

Applies to:

- Slow With Jesus: The Gospel of Matthew
- Slow With Jesus: The Gospel of Mark
- Slow With Jesus: The Gospel of Luke
- Slow With Jesus: The Gospel of John

## North Star

> You don't need another thing to read. You may need help slowing down with what you're already reading.

Brand promise:

**A slower way to meet Jesus in Scripture.**

Primary product descriptor:

**Guided Scripture Journal**

Secondary method signal:

**Inspired by Lectio Divina**

## Product job

Help Christians who already value Scripture stop rushing through it and stay with Gospel passages long enough to:

1. read slowly;
2. notice;
3. reflect;
4. journal;
5. pray;
6. respond.

The book is a guide for engaging Scripture, not another stream of Christian content to consume.

## Positioning boundaries

### More guidance than a blank Bible journal

The reader gets meaningful prompts and a repeatable path instead of an empty page.

### More participation than a devotional

The reader's interaction with Scripture is primary. Do not market the product as a collection of long author-written devotional readings.

### Less homework than a Bible-study workbook

Bible-study language is useful for discovery, but the primary experience should not sound academic, curriculum-heavy, video-dependent, or assignment-driven.

### More accessible than a Lectio-Divina-first product

Lectio Divina is an important influence and high-relevance niche. It is not the only front door.

**A Guided Scripture Journal with 24 Encounters for Reflection, Prayer, Journaling, and Response**

Never carry an encounter count from one Gospel into another until its final architecture is confirmed.

## Critical content correction

Do **not** position the current series around:

- a "Luke Lens";
- "Biblical Context" as a headline product feature;
- AI-generated Gospel-lens commentary.

The commercial promise is guided slow engagement with Scripture through reflection, prayer, journaling, and response.

## Keyword architecture

Use six search-intent layers:

1. **Gospel-specific** — e.g. `Gospel of Luke devotional`, `Luke Bible study journal`
2. **Product-format** — e.g. `guided Scripture journal`, `Bible reflection journal`
3. **Quiet-time / problem-led** — e.g. `quiet time with God journal`, `slow devotional for busy Christians`
4. **Method** — e.g. `Lectio Divina journal`, `Scripture meditation journal`
5. **Jesus / formation outcomes** — e.g. `encounters with Jesus devotional`, `spiritual formation journal`
6. **Brand problem language** — e.g. `stop rushing through Bible reading`, `slow down with God`

Do not treat estimated search volume as authoritative Amazon data.

## Core experiments

1. **Guided Scripture Journal vs Bible Study Journal**  
   Which search family produces better qualified buying traffic?

2. **Lectio Divina vs ordinary quiet-time language**  
   Does niche method language convert more efficiently?

3. **Problem-led message**  
   Test the brand hook: "You don't need another thing to read. You may need help slowing down with what you're already reading."

4. **Gospel-specific vs generic guided-journal searches**  
   Compare book-intent campaigns with format-intent campaigns.

5. **Product-target segment**  
   Compare Scripture-journal, Gospel-journal, Luke-study, and contemplative-adjacent shoppers.

6. **Series effect**  
   Once multiple volumes are live, measure cross-volume discovery and sales.

## Amazon listing copy rule

Lead with the reader problem before listing features.

Preferred first idea:

> Most of us do not need another Christian book to get through. We need help staying with Scripture long enough to notice what is there.

Then explain:

- 24 encounters for Luke;
- guided prompts;
- journaling space;
- reflection;
- prayer;
- response;
- inspired by Lectio Divina;
- self-paced use.

Do not keyword-stuff the description.

## A+ Content order

1. Stop rushing through Scripture.
2. Show the rhythm: Read → Notice → Reflect → Journal → Pray → Respond.
3. More guidance than a blank journal / more participation than a devotional / less homework than a Bible-study workbook.
4. Who the journal is for.
5. Inspired by Lectio Divina.
6. Gospel-series comparison / cross-sell once multiple volumes are live.

## Series reuse rule

For Matthew, Mark, and John:

- preserve the North Star;
- preserve `Guided Scripture Journal`;
- insert the actual encounter count;
- replace Luke-specific keyword and product targets;
- reuse proven general search terms only when accurate;
- create Gospel-specific exact campaigns;
- add each volume to the Amazon Series Page when eligible;
- use A+ comparison modules as the catalog grows.

## Deliberately deferred

Do not prioritize yet:

- church/bulk strategy;
- premium direct-sale edition;
- expansion beyond the Gospels.

First publish, market, gather actual buyer data, and improve the series from evidence.
